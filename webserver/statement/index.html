Background

Tyler, who worked on mitch.pro and later started Tylerium.pro, describes himself there as a former co-owner of mitch.pro who was denied credit and mistreated.

Tyler's role

Tyler was never the co-owner. He was added as a developer less than a year ago, not two as he claims, and later gave himself that title and I pushed off removing it. I didn't remove his permissions to upload code, I gave him more limited permissions because changes he shipped took the site down, compromised user privacy, and changed the site drastically in ways it shouldn't have. I limited him to pull requests, which is the same way I contribute myself. He shipped out "features" nearly weekly that broke the UI or was a sloppy "redesign".

Contribution

Tyler claims he spent two years on the site, in reality its been less than one. Even so, all time his contributions have been ~7% of the code, and of the code today he has contributed less than 4%.

How it was measured

Lines added over the history come from git log --numstat and count every line ever added, including code later rewritten or deleted. Lines in the codebase today come from git blame on the current tree, which attributes each remaining line to the commit that last changed it. The current count excludes lockfiles, images, fonts and other non-code files. Both use the commit author names on the public repository, with "Tyler Thompson" and "Codex" counted as Tyler's.

Tylerium.pro

Tylerium.pro is a sloppy copy of mitch.pro with core features removed. The UI problems of the past on mitch.pro are evident from who they came when you look at tylerium.pro.

Tyler's statement

I'm Tyler, the creator of Tylerium.pro and a former co-owner and developer of Mitch.pro. For nearly two years, I dedicated my time and effort to helping build, improve, and shape Mitch.pro into what it is today. Despite my contributions, I never felt I received the recognition or credit I deserved.

Over time, I went from co-owner to developer, eventually being demoted to a ridiculous rank called "Mitch's Scrotum." After everything I had contributed, I saw this as completely disrespectful.

That's why I created Tylerium.pro, not just as an improved alternative to Mitch.pro, but as a rebellion against how I was treated. Tylerium.pro represents my independence, my vision, and my determination to build something better without having my contributions overlooked.
