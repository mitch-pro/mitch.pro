Overview

This page lists every cookie and browser-storage item mitch.pro sets, what it's for, and how long it lasts. It's a companion to the Privacy Policy, which covers what account and activity information we collect more broadly — this page is specifically about the small pieces of data your browser holds on our behalf.

mitch.pro does not use advertising cookies, cross-site tracking cookies, or analytics cookies. Everything below exists to make the service work.

Essential cookies

These are required for the service to function — you can't be signed in without them.

  • Session cookie (mitch_session): proves you're signed in. Its value is a random token; only a one-way hash of it is stored on our servers, not the token itself. HttpOnly (not readable by page JavaScript), marked Secure on HTTPS, and expires after 30 days of being issued or on sign-out. A couple of short-lived derived cookies may also be set alongside it purely as an internal compatibility detail — they're recomputed from mitch_session on each request and carry no information beyond what it already represents.
  • theme: remembers your light/dark/preset display preference across visits. Read directly by page scripts to keep the UI consistent before the rest of the page loads; not sent anywhere beyond mitch.pro.

Local storage (not cookies, but similar)

localStorage entries live in your browser only — they're never automatically sent to the server the way cookies are, and we only read them when a page explicitly needs to.

  • Matrix chat session (access token, device ID, a few sync markers): keeps you signed into chat without a fresh login on every page load, and lets end-to-end encryption keep working across visits on the same device. Clearing your browser's site data for mitch.pro signs chat out and, for encrypted rooms, can require re-verifying your device.
  • Small UI state: things like which GIF-picker tab you last had open, or a dismissed banner's "don't show again" flag. Cosmetic only.

Third-party cookies

Google reCAPTCHA loads on specific forms where we need to tell a human apart from a bot (for example, account signup). While its script is active it can set its own cookies, under Google's own privacy practices, to compute a risk score. It is not loaded on pages that don't need it.

We do not use Google Analytics, advertising pixels, or any other cross-site tracking script. An analytics configuration variable exists in our deployment environment for possible future use, but nothing currently loads it — no analytics cookie is set today.

Why there's no cookie-consent banner

Consent banners exist to get permission for optional cookies — typically analytics or advertising. Since mitch.pro only sets cookies that are strictly necessary to run the service (plus reCAPTCHA, itself necessary for the specific form it protects), there's no optional tracking to ask permission for, so we don't show a banner asking for it.

Your choices

Your browser lets you block or delete cookies and site data at any time. Blocking the session cookie will sign you out and prevent signing back in; blocking local storage will sign chat out and may require re-verifying encrypted rooms on that device afterward. You can control the theme preference directly from Preferences instead of clearing cookies.

Changes to this page

If what we store changes, this page changes with it — the date at the top reflects the current version.
