Overview

mitch.pro respects intellectual property rights and expects members to do the same. This page explains how to report content you believe infringes your copyright — a shared canvas drawing, a chat message or attachment, a profile image, or anything else a member has posted — and what happens after you report it.

Filing a takedown notice

Send a notice to support@mitch.pro with the subject line "Copyright notice" and include all of the following:

  • A description of the copyrighted work you believe was infringed.
  • Where the infringing material appears on mitch.pro — a direct link, room name, or other information specific enough for us to locate it.
  • Your contact information: name, mailing address, phone number, and email address.
  • A statement that you have a good-faith belief the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  • A statement, made under penalty of perjury, that the information in your notice is accurate and that you are the copyright owner or authorized to act on their behalf.
  • Your physical or electronic signature.

Incomplete notices may take longer to act on, or may need to be returned for more information before we can process them.

Counter-notification

If content you posted was removed and you believe that was a mistake or misidentification, you can send a counter-notice to the same address with: the material removed and its location before removal, a statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good-faith belief it was removed in error, your contact information, a statement consenting to the jurisdiction of your local federal district court (or, if outside the United States, an appropriate judicial body), and your signature.

Repeat infringers

Accounts responsible for repeated, substantiated infringement are subject to restriction or termination at our discretion, independent of any other enforcement described in the Use Agreement.

Contact

Copyright notices and counter-notices: support@mitch.pro. General questions can go to the same address.

This page is an operational description, not legal advice. In particular, formally designating a DMCA agent with the U.S. Copyright Office (required for full safe-harbor protection under 17 U.S.C. § 512) is a separate step the site owner should take directly — this page alone doesn't complete that registration. Have counsel review this page, and the registration, before relying on either.
